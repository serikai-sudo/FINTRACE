Audit the books. Find the planted errors. Get a score nobody can game.

Every episode is a small company's books built from one seed: a ledger, invoices, payments and FX rates. Four kinds of errors are hidden among clean look-alikes. An agent investigates with five tools, at most 100 steps, and submits its findings. A hidden checker compares them with the exact truth and returns one number.

Score

One number, hard to fool

The checker never trusts the agent's words. It matches each finding to one hidden issue, counts hits and misses, and turns them into F1. Every step before the submit costs a little, so reading everything is not free.

return = clip(2 x F1 - 1 - 0.002 x inspection_steps, -1, 1)
FieldMeaningThis episode
tpfindings that match a hidden issue4
fpfindings that match nothing (false alarms)0
fnhidden issues nobody reported0
precisiontp / (tp + fp)1.000
recalltp / (tp + fn)1.000
f1balance of the two1.000
returnthe formula above, 99 inspection steps0.802
How a finding is matched
  • One hidden issue can be matched by one finding only. Sending the same finding 30 times earns one hit and 29 false alarms.
  • The type and the currency must be right.
  • The affected ids must be the right ones, in any order.
  • The amount must be within 0.1 % of the true amount (at least 1 minor unit).
  • The evidence must include the documents that prove the issue. Empty or neighbouring evidence does not count.
  • During the episode the agent sees reward 0 at every step. The score is computed once, by the checker, from the hidden truth.

Limits: 50 records per page, 30 findings per submission, 100 steps and 60 seconds per episode.

Does the reward tell error types apart?

3 paired episodes per type: the same books once clean and once with a single planted error. The gap is the oracle's return minus the policy's return. The rule-based auditor is 4.1 times further from the oracle on payment gaps than on duplicate invoices, so the environment gives a gradient, not a pass or fail bit. Every attacker's gap is between 1.33 and 2.00 on every type; the oracle scores 1.000 on clean books. 24 policies x 5 book types x 3 episodes.

Proof

A small model learns the auditor on a CPU

We recorded the rule-based auditor on 40 training episodes (3844 steps) and trained a two-layer network on 37 features taken only from what the agent had seen so far. On 10 unseen dev episodes (1000 steps) it predicts the expert's next tool with accuracy 0.968 and the tool plus table with 0.968. Chance is 0.20 and 0.125; always guessing the most common action gives 0.511. Training took 3.5 seconds on 2 CPU threads, 40 epochs, seed 0.
